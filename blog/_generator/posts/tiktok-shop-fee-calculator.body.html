TL;DR

  • TikTok's referral fee is 6% on most US categories. On a $60 order that is $3.60, the smallest line in the stack.
  • In our worked model, contribution margin lands at 17.3% on a $30 order, 23.2% on $60 and 25.7% on $120, after referral fee, affiliate commission, GMV Max cost, product cost and fulfillment.
  • Fixed fulfillment cost is what punishes low AOV. The same $5 pick-and-pack is 16.7% of a $30 order and 8.3% of a $120 one.
  • Affiliate rate and ad return move margin by 5 to 10 points or more. The platform fee never moves it.
  • Every rate except the 6% is an illustrative assumption. Swap in your own before you decide anything.

The TikTok Shop fee calculator we actually run

When a brand asks us how much it costs to sell on TikTok Shop, we do not answer with a fee percentage. We open a spreadsheet, build one order from the top, and see what is left. That left-over number, contribution margin per order, decides whether scaling spend on TikTok Shop is a good idea.

This post is that spreadsheet, written out. We covered the platform's published charges in TikTok Shop seller fees: what they actually cost at scale, so we will not restate the fee schedule here. This is the next step: put the fees next to affiliate commission, GMV Max cost, product cost and fulfillment, and see what a brand keeps.

The inputs, and where each one comes from

One input is sourced. Everything else is an assumption we chose so the example is readable, and you should replace it.

  • Referral fee: 6% (sourced). TikTok Shop's referral fee page on TikTok Shop Seller University sets 6% for most US categories, calculated as the rate times (customer payment plus platform discount minus tax). Some categories, such as select jewelry, differ, and TikTok can change the rate, so check Seller Center for yours.
  • Affiliate commission: 15% of order value (illustrative). You set this per product and creator tier. It is not a TikTok fee.
  • GMV Max cost: 20% of order value (illustrative). That is a 5.0 return on ad spend, applied to every order in the table. In practice only ad-attributed orders carry it, so read it as a fully paid-for order.
  • Product cost: 25% of price (illustrative).
  • Fulfillment: $5.00, $6.50 and $10.00 per order (illustrative), rising with weight and pack size. Free shipping to the customer is assumed.

Returns, refund administration fees, sample costs and any payment processing charge visible in your own settlement report are excluded to keep the table readable. Add them if they apply to you.

Three things stand out.

The platform fee is 6.0% of every order, and the creator and ad lines together are 35%. TikTok is not the expensive part of TikTok Shop.

Low AOV is where the model gets thin. At $30, fulfillment alone eats 16.7% of the order. Before you cut affiliate rate, look at whether you can raise the basket. A wellness supplements brand we work with lifted AOV through bundling, without leaning on discounts, and the same dollar costs take a smaller share of a bigger basket.

The last row matters more than it looks. An organic, non-ad order earns 20 points more in this model. That is why we treat ads as a way to buy reach on a product that already converts, not as the engine of the whole shop.

Operator note — Run this per SKU, not per shop. A hero product at $60 with a 25% product cost and a bundle at $120 with a 35% product cost have very different margins, and a blended number hides both. The shop-level figure is only useful once the SKU rows are right.

Every five points of commission is five points of margin. Dropping from a 5.0 to a 3.0 return costs 13.4 points. Neither is something TikTok charges you. Both are decisions.

That is why the commission structure deserves the attention most brands give the fee schedule. One health and wellness brand turned a single creator retainer into a 600% return by the three-week mark, through the rate negotiated before the deal started. And the affiliate side runs at a scale where small rate changes compound. Mike Begg of AMZ Advisers, our parent company, puts it this way:

Our best-performing brands are managing 200-500 active affiliates at any given time.

Mike Begg, co-founder and CEO of AMZ Advisers, in Why TikTok Shop Is the Biggest E-commerce Opportunity

At that count, a two-point change in blended commission is not a rounding error. It is the difference between a month that funds the next one and a month that does not.

Using the model to set an ad budget

The model also gives you a break-even return. At $60 with a 15% commission, the order breaks even on contribution when ad cost reaches 43.2% of order value, a return of about 2.3. Below that, GMV Max is losing money on that product. Above it, you are buying profitable orders. Our walkthrough of how to set up GMV Max covers the budget and ROI target side. A food and beverage brand we work with scaled its daily ad budget up 125% in roughly ten days, and it could do that because the unit economics were already known.

If you want to skip the spreadsheet, the interactive calculator in our seller fees post runs the same single-order math with your own inputs.

The platform fee is the one number everyone quotes and nobody can change. The two you can change are the ones that decide the margin, and they are worth modeling before the first dollar of spend.
