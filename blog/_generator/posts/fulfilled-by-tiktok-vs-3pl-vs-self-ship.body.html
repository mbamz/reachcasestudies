The honest answer is that Fulfilled by TikTok (FBT), a third-party logistics provider (3PL) and shipping it yourself are three different ways of deciding who carries the risk on your account — and the cheapest one on paper is not always the cheapest one in practice.

This is a decision guide for brands that already have inventory, a warehouse relationship and volume. We pulled every platform number below from TikTok Shop's own Seller University pages, and where TikTok hasn't published a figure we say so rather than hand you one.

Fulfilled by TikTok (FBT): what it is and what it costs

FBT is TikTok Shop's in-house fulfillment service. You send inventory to TikTok's fulfillment network, and TikTok stores, picks, packs and ships it. Eligible items carry a Free 3-Day Delivery tag. Per TikTok's FBT overview, that covers over 80% of U.S. orders, 98% of orders ship within one calendar day, and every inbound shipment gets 60 days of free storage.

On cost, TikTok's own positioning is a fulfillment cost reduction of 20% to 35% versus mainstream carriers. Treat that as TikTok's claim about its own service, not a number for your catalog. What matters for your model is the structure of the FBT rate card, which charges per unit for pick, pack, packaging and last-mile shipping:

  • Fulfillment fees vary by weight tier and units per order. A new rate card took effect January 12, 2026; single-unit fees for 16 to 50 lb items dropped on May 20, multi-unit fees for 5 to 20 lb items rose on June 18, and the card extended to items up to 150 lb on July 13.
  • Storage is free for the first 60 days, then charged in tiers up to 270 days. TikTok reduced those tiers on December 15, 2025; storage beyond 270 days was left unchanged.
  • Free shipping is the default on FBT orders. Sellers can opt out, in which case buyers pay $5.99 on orders under $30 and the seller receives $0.75 per unit.
  • Returns and incident fees are itemized per unit, and inbound non-compliance carries its own fee.

We are deliberately not quoting a per-unit dollar rate. TikTok has moved the card in both directions this year, and the current table is the one in your Seller Center.

TikTok fulfillment and account health: where the real risk sits

This is the part most fulfillment comparisons skip, and it's the part that matters most to a brand with something to lose. Under TikTok's fulfillment policy, a regular order has to reach In Transit within 2 business days of Awaiting Shipment, and gets auto-cancelled at 5 business days without tracking. The numbers that govern your shop:

  • Late Dispatch Rate (LDR): 4% or lower is recommended, and enforcement starts above 10%.
  • On-Time Delivery Rate (OTDR): 80% or higher.
  • Valid Tracking Rate (VTR): 95% or higher, for seller-shipped orders.
  • Seller-Fault Cancellation Rate: 2.5% or lower.

Miss them and the consequences stack: account health rating point deductions, reduced product visibility, listing removal, order volume limits and, in the worst case, deactivation. That is the same enforcement ladder we walk through in how long TikTok Shop violations last.

FBT orders are largely exempt from these logistics metrics, which is the cleanest argument for FBT on high-velocity SKUs. A 3PL or self-ship does not remove the metrics. It hands the work of hitting them to a provider you hired, on a shop you own.

Operator note — A 3PL's scan is your dispatch event. "In Transit" needs a real carrier acceptance, not a printed label, and a warehouse that batches pickups late on a Friday will quietly push orders past the SLA. Ask any 3PL for its TikTok order cut-off and its weekend and federal-holiday handling before you sign. TikTok's business-day math skips both.

TikTok Shop FBT: when it fits and when it doesn't

We lean toward FBT when three things are true: the SKU turns fast enough that the 60 free storage days are real, the catalog is mostly standard size and weight, and the shop is exposed to demand spikes. Demand on TikTok Shop is not evenly distributed. A breath-mint brand pushed GMV Max budget into a promo week and GMV jumped 111% week over week, and a spike like that is exactly when a warehouse that packs on a fixed daily schedule starts missing a 2-day SLA.

FBT is a weaker fit for slow movers (storage fees compound), anything oversize or fragile, and any brand that needs custom packaging or kitting that the inbound requirements won't allow. It also means moving inventory away from your other channels, so you need the sell-through to justify splitting stock.

When a 3PL or self-ship still wins

A 3PL makes sense when you already run omnichannel inventory from one pool and your provider can hit a 2-day dispatch consistently. You keep branded packaging, you keep one inventory number, and you control cost per order through your own contract. The price is that you own the TikTok dispatch metrics, so you need a daily check on LDR.

Self-ship is the narrowest option. Seller shipping is available to select sellers only, and it asks for manual tracking and full responsibility for delivery performance. We see it as a fit where an operator has a custom carrier arrangement (large, heavy or regulated goods) that FBT's rate card can't match.

Why the account-health side deserves weight: a pet supplies brand in a heavily policed category hit $79K in May GMV against a $70K goal while absorbing a near-constant stream of violations. What held that account together was fast appeals and a close line to the platform. A fulfillment model that adds late-dispatch penalties to that pile makes the same job harder.

How we decide with brands: five questions

  1. Can your current provider hit 2-day dispatch for TikTok orders, every day of the week? If not, look at FBT or a faster 3PL.
  2. What's your landed cost per order today, including packaging, pick and pack, and carrier? Compare that to the FBT rate card for your weight tier, not to the headline savings range.
  3. How fast does the hero SKU turn? Past 60 days of storage, FBT economics change.
  4. How tight is your account health today? A shop already near an enforcement milestone has the most to gain from FBT's metric exclusion.
  5. Do you need branded packaging or kitting? If yes, check the FBT packaging requirements before you plan around it.
