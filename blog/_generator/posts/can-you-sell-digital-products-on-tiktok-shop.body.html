The short answer

Yes, but not the way most brands picture it when they ask. TikTok Shop does not let a seller casually list a PDF, a course, or a digital download the way you'd list a physical SKU. Digital and virtual products are only sellable through a dedicated Virtual Goods category, and that category is invite-only. Outside of it, TikTok Shop's own Prohibited Products Policy is direct: "Virtual or digital products are prohibited unless approved for sale under the Virtual Goods category." We pulled the current rules straight from TikTok's Seller Center documentation, because this is exactly the kind of policy that gets paraphrased into something looser than it actually is.

TL;DR

  • Digital and virtual products are prohibited on TikTok Shop by default — they're only sellable through the Virtual Goods category, and that category is invite-only.
  • Approved virtual goods (where TikTok has extended access) include software licenses, digital games, in-game content, prepaid subscriptions, online courses, virtual gift cards, and virtual event tickets.
  • Every virtual goods listing carries a $500 price cap, and sales of $10,000+ to one customer in 24 hours aren't permitted.
  • Virtual goods must be delivered electronically within 2 business days, and TikTok requires redemption to happen outside TikTok Shop itself.
  • Listing a digital product as if it were physical, shipping an empty box, or rewriting the description to dodge review is a policy violation, not a workaround.
  • Physical products that happen to carry digital content — a DVD, a printed guide with a code inside — are still permitted normally, because they're physical listings first.

What the Virtual Goods category actually allows

TikTok Shop's Virtual Goods Requirements page lays out a narrower lane than "digital products" as a category name suggests. It isn't open self-serve enrollment — a seller has to be invited and approved before a single virtual listing can go live. Once approved, the permitted product types are specific, not a general digital-goods catch-all:

  • Software and applications, sold as a digital license key or activation code
  • Digital games, sold as full-version redemption codes
  • In-game content and DLC, via redemption code or account transfer
  • Game subscriptions and memberships, prepaid only — no auto-renewal
  • Other digital subscriptions, also prepaid only
  • Online courses, pre-recorded or live, delivered digitally
  • Virtual gift cards, for approved platforms
  • Virtual and concert tickets, as digital passes or redemption codes

Every one of those still runs inside real limits. Here's the rule set as TikTok's own Seller Center documents it:

What's still flatly prohibited

Outside an approved Virtual Goods listing, TikTok Shop's Prohibited Products Policy treats digital and virtual items as blocked, full stop — and it names specific categories, not just a general caution. Custom document creation, exam-related solutions and ghost-writing services, and unauthorized streaming or circumvention tools are called out by name, alongside the broader "virtual or digital products" prohibition.

Operator note — The policy is explicit that trying to route around the review process is its own violation, separate from whether the product itself would ever qualify. TikTok's language: don't list a digital item as a physical product, don't ship an empty package, and don't rewrite a description to avoid review. Any of those reads as misrepresentation, and the enforcement path is listing removal, loss of the ability to offer products for sale, and a hit to Account Health Rating — not a warning to try again quieter.

There's one exception worth knowing if your catalog already sits in physical goods, which most 8- and 9-figure TikTok Shop brands do: "Physical products such as DVDs with digital content are permitted." A physical item that happens to carry or unlock digital content is still a physical listing. That's a meaningfully different thing than trying to sell the digital content on its own.

Source: TikTok Shop Seller Center — Prohibited Products Policy.

Where this actually matters for a brand already doing real volume

Most brands asking this question aren't trying to build a course business. They're asking because they've got a physical catalog already moving on TikTok Shop and they're wondering whether a digital add-on — a guide, a code, a bonus — is a fast way to add a new revenue line without touching fulfillment. The honest read: for a brand without an existing Virtual Goods invitation, it isn't a fast lane. It's a locked door, and the policy cost of trying to force it (a suspended listing, a health-score hit) is higher than the upside of one extra SKU.

What we watch work instead is the same lever TikTok Shop already rewards inside the catalog you can list today: a new product, tested properly. A crochet-kit brand on our roster was running around $500 a month in GMV on its owned-IP catalog alone. We added one licensed-IP kit beside the existing line — not a new category, not a digital SKU, just a better-tested physical product — and GMV crossed $1,500 that month, up 150% month over month before the month even closed. The growth tracked the new kit directly.

The other lever that outperforms a locked digital-goods door is what you charge per order, not what you add to the catalog. A wellness supplements brand we work with lifted AOV from $35.93 to $40.07 in a single month, an 11.52% gain, using bundles and cart-building promotions instead of item discounts. Neither of those moves needed an invitation from TikTok. Both are open to any seller running a normal catalog today.

The bigger lever is distribution, not the SKU type

Whether a listing is physical or (rarely) virtual, what actually moves it on TikTok Shop is the same mechanism: creators putting it in front of buyers who weren't searching for it.

That's the piece worth building toward before a digital SKU is even on the table. A creator bench sourced and seeded on a real cadence is what turns any new product — licensed-IP kit, bundle, or the rare approved virtual good — into GMV instead of a listing nobody sees. That's the part of the platform every seller has access to on day one, invite or not.

If TikTok Shop does eventually extend a Virtual Goods invitation to your account, the rules above are what to build the listing against — price cap, delivery window, and all. Until then, the fastest real lever for an 8- or 9-figure brand isn't a digital SKU behind a locked door. It's running the catalog you already have through a creator bench that's actually working.
