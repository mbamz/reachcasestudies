How Long Do TikTok Shop Violations Last? And How to Keep Selling Through One

The point deduction and the enforcement action run on two different clocks, and most advice online only describes one of them. Here is what TikTok Shop's own Seller Center documentation says about the 180-day window, the 30-day and 15-day appeal deadlines, and what actually keeps GMV moving while a case is open.

The short answer

A violation notice lands in Seller Center, the shop owner forwards it to us, and the first question is always the same one: how long do TikTok Shop violations last? The honest answer has two halves, and most of the advice floating around only gives you one of them. The point deduction sits in a rolling 180-day window on your Account Health Rating and ages out of that window on its own. The enforcement action attached to it — a pulled listing, a paused campaign, a suspended ad account — does not wait 180 days and does not clear itself. It clears when you appeal it or when its stated duration runs out, whichever comes first. Treating those two clocks as one number is how brands end up sitting on a dead listing for six months that a 30-day appeal would have brought back.

TL;DR

  • TikTok Shop's Account Health Rating (AHR) runs on a rolling 180-day window. Point deductions age out of that window; they are not a permanent mark.
  • Every seller starts at 200 points on a 0–1,000 scale. The milestones that bite are 150, 100, 50 and 0.
  • A first appeal is due within 30 calendar days of the violation notice. A second appeal is due within 15 calendar days of the first decision. You get two, then it is closed.
  • The 90-day figure you will see quoted is real but it is a different screen: it is the violation view for the creator accounts linked to your shop, not your seller AHR.
  • You can earn points back — 4 points per 200 finished orders, capped at 20 a week — and passing the policy quiz tagged to a violation can shorten the enforcement.
  • GMV does not have to stop while any of this runs. A pet supplies brand we work with beat a $70,000 May goal by $9,000 through a near-constant stream of violations and bans.

How long do TikTok Shop violations last? The 180-day window, precisely

TikTok Shop scores seller compliance through the Account Health Rating, a color-coded number from 0 to 1,000 calculated, in TikTok's own wording, "based on your shop's activities over the last 180 days." Every seller starts at 200 points. Policy and performance breaches deduct points, scaled to severity; finished orders and passed policy quizzes add them back. Because the window rolls, a deduction taken today stops counting against you roughly six months from today, assuming nothing new lands in between.

What actually hurts is not the deduction itself. It is crossing a milestone. Here is what TikTok's Seller Center says happens at each one:

AHR milestone What TikTok Shop restricts How long
200 points Starting score for every new seller — no restrictions —
150 points May not be able to create new listings or enroll in new mega campaigns 7 days
100 points Same restrictions, longer hold 14 days
50 points Same restrictions, longer hold again 28 days
0 points Account may be permanently deactivated Permanent

Source: TikTok Shop Seller Center — Account Health Rating (AHR) Requirements.

Two things to read carefully in that table. First, the restriction durations are 7, 14 and 28 days — they are not 180. A brand that drops to 150 is locked out of new listings for a week, not half a year. Second, the wording is "may not be able to," because TikTok reserves discretion. Plan against the stated duration; do not treat it as a contract.

Operator note — The 180-day window is why a clean quarter is worth real money on this platform. Points do not just sit there. Every month you run without a new deduction, older deductions are falling out of the back of the window while finished orders add points at the front. Account health is a flow, not a scar.

Do TikTok Shop violations go away? Yes, but three different things are expiring

This is where the conflicting answers online come from. People are describing three separate mechanics and calling all of them "the violation."

  • The AHR point deduction ages out of the rolling 180-day seller window. This is the one that answers "how long do TikTok Shop violations last" for a shop owner.
  • Violation records on linked creator accounts run on a shorter view. TikTok's Account Health page documentation states plainly: "You can only view violations from the past 90 days. Violations older than 90 days will be automatically reset and removed." That is a different screen for a different entity. If someone quoted you 90 days, this is almost certainly what they were reading.
  • The enforcement action — the removed listing, the blocked campaign enrollment, the suspended ad account — runs on its own stated duration, or until a successful appeal reverses it. It is not tied to either window.

The practical consequence: waiting is a strategy for the point deduction and a mistake for the enforcement action. The point will expire without you. The dead listing will not.

You also do not have to wait passively on the score. TikTok lets sellers earn points back at 4 points for every 200 finished orders, capped at 20 points per week, and by taking and passing the policy quiz attached to a violation. For a brand doing real volume, that order-based recovery is not trivial — it is a reason to keep the shop selling rather than freeze everything out of caution.

How to appeal a TikTok Shop violation, and the two windows that matter

The appeal path is short and the deadlines are hard. From TikTok Shop's own appeals documentation:

Step Deadline Where
First appeal Within 30 calendar days after the violation notification is sent Seller Center → Shop Health → Violation Records → View & Appeal → Appeal
Second appeal (if the first is denied) Within 15 calendar days after the appeal decision is sent Same path
Third appeal Not available — two is the limit —

What you submit with it matters as much as when. TikTok asks for a clear explanation of why you believe the enforcement action was taken in error, plus any relevant supporting documents, images or videos, plus anything else TikTok Shop specifically requests on the case. And the policy closes with a line worth internalizing before you write anything emotional: "All decisions made by TikTok Shop are at our sole discretion and are final."

Source: TikTok Shop Seller Center — How to appeal a violation.

The operating rule we run for clients is simple and it is mostly about speed. Appeal the same day the notice lands. Not because 30 days is tight, but because the enforcement action is live the whole time the appeal is open, and every day a listing sits unlisted is a day of GMV that does not come back. The 30-day window is the outside edge of what is allowed, not a schedule to work to.

What changed on August 17, 2026

TikTok Shop updated how some policy violations feed into AHR effective August 17, 2026. Additional violation types can now affect AHR, some violations deduct more points depending on type and severity, and violation notices now show the score impact directly. TikTok's own note is explicit that the framework around it did not move: tier definitions, enforcement thresholds, the 180-day point window and the appeal process all stayed the same. So if you read a guide written before that date, the timelines are still right; the point weights may not be.

Source: TikTok Shop Seller Center — Policy Pulse, August 2026.

Keeping GMV growing through a violation, not around it

Everything above is the mechanics. The part that decides whether a violation costs you a month or a quarter is what the shop is doing while the appeal is open.

Some categories get policed harder than others. If you sell pet supplies, supplements, or anything with a health or safety claim attached, enforcement is not an event you occasionally absorb. It is the operating environment. A pet supplies brand on our roster finished May at $79,000 in GMV against a $70,000 goal — $9,000 over, 13% above target — through a near-constant stream of violations and account bans. The beat is the headline; the conditions are the proof. Three habits produced it: keep GMV Max campaigns optimized so the paid engine never goes idle, hold a working line with the platform account manager so issues get escalated rather than queued, and appeal every violation the moment it lands so downtime is measured in hours instead of days.

The cost of letting the engine go dark is measurable, and it is larger than people expect. A food and beverage brand had GMV Max offline for more than three weeks because of a Business Center subaccount disconnect. The reconnect brought the campaigns back live the same day. GMV moved 112% week over week off the same campaigns that had been sitting there the whole time. Nothing about the creative changed. The only variable was whether the account was allowed to run.

The checklist we run when a violation lands

  • Read the notice for the score impact. Since August 2026 the point deduction is shown on the notice itself. That number tells you how close a milestone is before you decide how hard to fight it.
  • Check the current AHR against 150. That is the first milestone with teeth. If you are within one more deduction of it, the appeal is urgent rather than routine.
  • Appeal the same day, with evidence attached. Explanation of the error, supporting documents, and anything the case specifically asks for. One shot, then one more, then it is final.
  • Take the policy quiz tagged to the violation. It adds points back and can shorten the enforcement duration.
  • Do not pause the parts of the shop that are still running. Paid, affiliates and Live are usually unaffected by a single listing enforcement. Finished orders are also how the score recovers.
  • Escalate through your platform account manager. A real relationship there is the difference between an issue that moves and a ticket that sits.

None of that is glamorous. Together it is the difference between a violation that costs a brand a Tuesday and one that costs it a quarter. It is also most of what our TikTok Shop account health and appeals work actually consists of: fast, boring, repeatable responses that keep the selling surface live while the paperwork resolves.

One last thing worth saying plainly, because it sets expectations correctly. A violation is not a verdict on the brand. On a rolling 180-day window with points that come back through ordinary order volume, a single deduction is a cost of doing business in a policed category — and the brands that grow fastest in those categories are not the ones who never get flagged. They are the ones who built a shop that keeps selling while the flag is being cleared, at a cost structure that still works when a week of listings goes missing.
